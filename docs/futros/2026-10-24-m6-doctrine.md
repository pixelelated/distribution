### Pre-mortem

It is 2026-11-07. T4's punch list had 140 items; 120 were Low wording and
clarity items; the coordinator wrote eleven stream briefs over four days
while T3's and T5's hand-backs waited; two streams edited
`engineering-practices.md` and `device-builds.md` in paragraphs the

found what it should, and the remediation was sized by the finding count
instead of by the ladder*. Prevention, already in the brief and sharpened by
§ 5: a Low `Renovation` item that is wording rides the audit issue and is
fixed in **one** stream per file (not per item); a rule wrong twice goes to a
tool proposal (an issue), not to a rewrite stream; the coordinator's rule
edits between stage A and the merge are posted and listed as they happen
(A7); and the merge's union is rehearsed on a throwaway branch before the
real one, as T1's acceptance did.

A second ending: T4 finds that the doctrine corpus is **5,179 lines a session
cannot hold** and proposes, under Q5, moving a third of it behind narrower
globs; the maintainer accepts; the next lane loads 3,000 lines and misses a
rule that would have stopped a device action. The ladder answers it: the
rules that gate safety (`engineering-practices.md` § Nothing runs on a
person's device, `vm-first.md`, the reboot rule) are **routed** every
session by decision (D-QA-015's weight), and a Q5 proposal names the
safety rules it does not touch before it names the lines it saves. **The
replaced-mechanism archetype** applies here: what an every-session rule
*guards* is that it cannot be skipped by not opening a file; a glob is the
mechanism that replaces that guard, and the stream brief lists, for each
rule moved, who would have read it and now will not.
